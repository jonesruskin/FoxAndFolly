# Facing East

**Closing-card subtitle:** *The sunflower who stopped chasing the sun.*
**Closing-card hook:** *Next summer, find a sunflower field at dawn. Look east.*
**Runtime:** 4:37 (277.0 s) · 1920×1080 · 30 fps · no voiceover, 28 on-screen narration lines

## Logline

A young sunflower named Hesper chases the Sun across the sky every day and pities the old flowers who stand frozen, faces to the east. When she blooms, her stem stiffens and she can't turn any more. She spends one long night sure that she has given up, until the dawn rises straight into her face and she learns what the old ones knew.

## Moral

**Not all stillness is surrender.** Chasing isn't the only way to reach what you love. Sometimes wisdom means learning where the light returns, and being there when it does.

## The twist

The story sets up the old flowers as a cautionary picture of resignation: heavy, motionless, "as if the sky had never moved". The dawn shows the reverse. Facing east is the best position in the field. They're the first to be warmed and the first to be found, by the light and by the bees. The opening image ("Every night, when no one was watching, Hesper turned around") also gets reframed. Her body had been learning to face the dawn all along.

## Real-world anchor (verified; sources in `production_notes.md`)

- Young sunflowers track the sun east→west during the day and swing back to face east at night, driven by an internal circadian clock.
- At maturity (when the flower opens) the stem stops tracking and most heads face east permanently.
- In the 2016 study, east-facing heads warmed faster in the morning and received about **five times more pollinator visits** than west-facing heads. The on-screen line attributes this to the study, because a 2024 field study in commercial plantations did not find the same effect (see notes).
- Hidden detail: every sunflower disc in the film is drawn with the real floret arrangement. Floret *k* sits at angle *k* × 137.5° (the golden angle), which produces the Fibonacci spirals of a real sunflower head.
- Hidden detail: *Hesper* means "evening" / "the west" (Hesperus, the evening star). A flower named for the west learns to face east.
