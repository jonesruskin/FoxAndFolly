# Fox & Folly — Channel Bible

**Tagline:** Old wisdom. Sharp twists.
**Audience:** teens and adults. Emotionally mature, thoughtful, never childish or preachy.
**Format:** original fables, 3:30–4:45, told through non-human characters (creatures, elements,
objects, celestial bodies, plants, animals). Each carries one clear but subtle moral.

## What makes an episode ours
1. **An original fable.** Never a retelling. Public-domain inspiration only if it is transformed into something clearly new.
2. **No voiceover.** The story is told through 20–30 short poetic on-screen lines.
3. **Rhythm and repetition.** Short sentences in parallel pairs ("Thera had never seen the sky." / "Ligeia had never seen a star.").
4. **Hook in the first 10 seconds.** Usually a mystery line that the ending answers.
5. **Emotional arc:** setup → longing or conflict → turning point → resolution.
6. **A twist that reframes the story.** The ending should make the viewer re-read the opening.
7. **The moral emerges from the story.** At most, it's stated once as the last story line.
8. **Real science woven in.** At least one true, verifiable fact, then 2–3 fact lines after the story, ending with a small real-world call to action ("Look up." / "Look east.").
9. **Closing title card:** title, one-line subtitle, the hook line, and the channel mark.

## Narrative voice
- Present the world plainly. Let the images carry the emotion.
- Prefer concrete nouns and verbs to abstractions. One metaphor per line at most.
- Characters get names with hidden meanings, which make good material for the pinned comment.
- Avoid: sarcasm, modern slang, lecturing, exclamation marks, "and they lived happily…".

## Recurring deliverables per episode
`final_video.mp4`, `subtitles.en.srt`, `script.md`, `music.wav`, `thumbnail_frame.png` (1280×720),
`youtube_upload.md` (title, description with hook, summary, fact note, comment question, subscribe line,
chapters and hashtags; tags; pinned comment), and `production_notes.md`.
